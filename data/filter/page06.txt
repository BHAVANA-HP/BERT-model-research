Insulin Regimens and Blood Glucose Control in Type 1 Diabetes

Patients living with Type 1 Diabetes require lifelong exogenous insulin replacement because autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells eliminates endogenous insulin production. Modern glycemic management involves intensive basal-bolus insulin regimens administered via multiple daily subcutaneous injections or continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion pumps. Long-acting basal insulin analogs like glargine maintain steady baseline glucose suppression overnight and between meals, while rapid-acting analogs like lispro are titrated before meals based on carbohydrate counting and current glycemia. Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) provide real-time interstitial sensor readings, alerting patients to acute hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia. Strict glycemic oversight diminishes the long-term risk of diabetic microvascular complications, including retinopathy, nephropathy, and peripheral neuropathy.
