Seasonal Allergy Symptoms and Management

Seasonal allergic rhinitis, commonly known as hay fever, affects millions of individuals every spring and autumn when pollen counts rise. Typical clinical symptoms include persistent sneezing bouts, clear nasal discharge, itchy conjunctiva, and nasal mucosal congestion. When airborne allergens enter the respiratory tract, sensitized mast cells release histamines and other inflammatory mediators that trigger mucosal irritation. Over-the-counter second-generation oral antihistamines, such as cetirizine and loratadine, provide symptom relief without causing severe drowsiness. Intranasal corticosteroid sprays are frequently recommended by primary care physicians as first-line maintenance therapy for moderate to severe symptoms. Patients experiencing wheezing, shortness of breath, or secondary sinus infections should schedule an evaluation with a board-certified allergist to explore immunotherapy or targeted treatment regimens.
