Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Generalized Anxiety Disorder

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is an established evidence-based psychological treatment for patients diagnosed with generalized anxiety disorder and panic symptoms. Delivered across twelve to twenty structured weekly clinical sessions by licensed clinical psychologists, CBT focuses on identifying, examining, and modifying maladaptive cognitive distortions and habitual catastrophic worry. Patients learn cognitive restructuring techniques to reframe irrational anticipated threats and engage in gradual in-vivo exposure exercises to desensitize autonomic hyperarousal. Somatic relaxation skills, such as diaphragmatic breathing and progressive muscle relaxation, help mitigate persistent physiological tension. Randomized controlled clinical trials consistently demonstrate that structured psychotherapy achieves sustained clinical remission and reduces reliance on psychotropic medications.
