Nutritional Therapy and Medical Management of Celiac Disease

Celiac disease is a chronic systemic autoimmune enteropathy triggered by the ingestion of gluten peptides in genetically predisposed individuals. When gluten proteins from wheat, barley, or rye reach the small intestine, they stimulate mucosal inflammation and progressive blunting of the intestinal villi, severely compromising nutrient absorption. Left untreated, patients suffer chronic malabsorption, iron-deficiency anemia, osteopenia, and unexplained weight loss. The only established therapeutic intervention is strict, lifelong adherence to a gluten-free diet. Gastroenterologists and specialized clinical dietitians monitor intestinal mucosal healing via periodic serological antibody titers and duodenal biopsies. Patients must be educated on cross-contamination risks and receive targeted supplementation for common micronutrient deficiencies including folate, zinc, and fat-soluble vitamins.
