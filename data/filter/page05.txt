Migraine Headaches: Phases, Triggers, and Treatment

Migraine is a complex neurological disorder characterized by recurrent attacks of moderate to severe throbbing head pain, predominantly unilateral, often lasting between four and seventy-two hours. Many migraine sufferers experience an initial prodrome phase featuring neck stiffness, fatigue, and mood fluctuations, followed in some cases by an aura involving visual scotomas or sensory paresthesias. During the acute headache phase, intense cephalic pain is frequently accompanied by nausea, vomiting, photophobia, and phonophobia. Treatment strategies comprise acute abortive medications, such as triptans or gepants taken at headache onset, alongside preventive therapies including beta-blockers, antiepileptics, or calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) monoclonal antibodies for high-frequency patients. Neurologists recommend maintaining a detailed headache log to isolate environmental and dietary triggers.
